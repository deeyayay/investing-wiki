## Investment Thesis
Sivers Semiconductors operates two divisions: Photonics (InP laser arrays for co-packaged optics) and Wireless (mmWave beamforming transceivers for 5G backhaul, radar, defense, and SATCOM). The photonics division is the primary AI thesis: as hyperscaler GPU clusters push interconnect speeds beyond copper's thermal and latency limits, co-packaged optics (CPO) replaces copper with integrated optical chiplets — and every CPO module requires an external light source (ELS). Sivers designs the InP laser arrays that serve as that ELS. The company reported FY2025 revenue of SEK 304M (~$33M USD), up 25% YoY, with Q4 accelerating at +17% sequential. Not yet profitable; management targets 2028 for breakeven.

The bull case is CPO adoption timing and the asset-light model. Sivers uses outsourced manufacturing (Jabil partnership confirmed), meaning revenue can scale without proportional capex. The March 2026 O-Net/Enablence partnership — developing ELS modules for AI datacenter CPO rollout — is the first clear signal of commercial traction beyond design-phase. The strategic LiDAR customer is the near-term revenue catalyst: targeting Q4 2026 high-volume production ramp, with $53–138M in lifecycle value ($5–14M annualized at steady state, or ~15–40% of current annual revenue). The opportunity pipeline grew 64% in 2025 to $453M — most not yet contracted, but directionally validating customer engagement.

The risk picture is equally specific. Cash runway is under 12 months at current burn — the company has raised twice in 12 months (equity + $17M in Feb 2026 via term loan and convertible debt) and shares outstanding grew 32% YoY. The stock trades at 31x P/S vs. a peer average of 17.5x, pricing in flawless execution on a 2x–3x revenue ramp. The leadership team is newly assembled (CEO July 2024, CFO Sept 2025 — combined Sivers tenure under 2 years). Achilles Capital, the largest individual shareholder, has its parent (DDM Finance) in restructuring — a forced-sale overhang. A criminal investigation and a delayed annual report add governance risk that disqualifies this from a core position at current risk/reward.

**Key moat:** InP laser design expertise with proprietary on-wafer coating enabling high manufacturing yield; Jabil manufacturing partnership (tech-to-hyperscale validation); early CPO design-wins (O-Net/Enablence); mmWave + photonics duality provides two independent revenue legs.

**Key risks:** Sub-12-month cash runway (ongoing dilution risk), 31x P/S valuation (punishes any execution miss), newly assembled leadership team (track record at Sivers unknown), Achilles Capital distress overhang, criminal investigation and delayed annual report (governance red flag), competitive displacement from silicon photonics or hyperscaler vertical integration.

### The one thing that matters

The demand leg got materially stronger and is now corroborated by a primary source outside the
company: AXT's InP substrate revenue nearly tripled year-on-year on datacenter demand, and the
largest merchant player in the field is publicly 30% short. But the two legs that made SIVE
attractive *as a business model* — asset-light scaling and a funding constraint that would resolve
into operating leverage — both changed shape this quarter. Sivers is now a capitalised company
building capacity into declining reported revenue. That is a different investment than the one
this page described in May.
