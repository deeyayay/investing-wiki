$SIVE spent its earnings call explaining why a company with falling revenue is about to start building fabs. The reason management gave was a quote from someone else. Lumentum's CEO said in July that the indium phosphide shortage could end up worse than memory.

That is the frame for this quarter, and it is a bigger change than the numbers.

Sivers is moving photonics from fab-light to what management called a hybrid manufacturing model, expanding the Glasgow site. The rationale given was that qualified merchant InP laser capacity is scarce and increasingly supply constrained, that laser demand outstrips supply for the next three to five years, and that generational shifts in optical interconnect are arriving faster than planned. In the Q&A the target mix came out: about one third of capacity from inside Sivers, two thirds from partners. A company that spent years positioning as a designer is buying process control.

Read that against what Nvidia filed the same week. Nvidia raised supply commitments from $119bn to $279bn in a single quarter, primarily for memory, and guided gross margin to a 71-72% trough partly on memory pricing. If the InP gap is genuinely wider than the memory gap, the same rent transfer is running one layer over, in a market with a fraction of the capital behind it.

On the balance sheet the CFO said out loud what the filing only implied. The larger June raise and the loan conversion completed in July, so neither appears in the quarter-end position. About SEK 825m of equity raised in total, the Bootstrap convertible converted, the remaining USD 5.0m term loan repaid, and no interest-bearing debt left. Net position moved from roughly SEK 100m of net debt at 30 June to about SEK 763m of net cash. His summary was that they no longer need to focus on funding the transformation.

Two details I had not seen anywhere. First, the social security accrual behind the SEK 42.9m charge is hedged: Sivers holds treasury shares to be sold when options are exercised, and those proceeds offset the actual payments, so the cash cost is pre-funded even though the charge swings reported EBITDA. Second, reserving future partner capacity carries no fee. Management was asked directly and said the arrangement works through strategic partnership rather than payments between the parties. For a company with negative operating cash flow, capacity that costs nothing to hold matters.

Then the pipeline, where the call added the number that reframes it. Asked how much of the USD 1.2bn could be captured and by when, management said it is the 26 through 30 pipeline. Five years, and a rolling window: once 2026 is done it drops out and the next five years are shown. Spread evenly that is USD 240m a year against roughly USD 24m of current annualised revenue. Ten times the business, which is the real bull case, and a different object from the headline number being quoted around.

A shareholder did press on this, asking why the pipeline rolls forward without converting. Management said it wanted to correct the questioner, that conversions are the production orders, and that they are reported as they land. That is a fair answer on its own terms. It is also the closest anyone got to the balance sheet, and it went to orders rather than to collection.

Because across 52 minutes and 6,500 words, contract assets came up zero times. Collection and receivables, zero. The Note 6 restatement, zero. Cash flow, once. Contract assets sit at SEK 109.3m against SEK 62.7m of reported cash, up 86% year on year while sales fell, and no post-period financing event touches that line. It is the number that decides whether 2027 is an operating story or an equity-market story.

One more gap worth flagging. Asked about longer-term blended margins as product revenue takes over from NRE, management pointed back to the financial model shown at the Q4 2025 webinar rather than giving an updated target. Gross margin came up twice in the whole call.

Against that, the LiDAR order looks credible rather than hopeful. Management said the visibility comes from customer production demands, forecasts and order placement timelines, and pointed to the ALL.SPACE order flagged the same way last quarter and since delivered at USD 8.2m. That is a checkable claim with a track record attached, which is more than most guidance offers.

The rest, briefly. Dual listing preparation targets completion in H1 2027, after which management framed it as market conditions rather than readiness. CPO is taking longer than hoped, so the near-term focus has shifted to pluggables and NPO. Revenue was also held back by government-related delays carried from last quarter. The USD 4bn SOA opportunity for optical circuit switching is explicitly early days. A JPMorgan photonics report listed Sivers alongside Lumentum and Coherent, which the CEO called humbling.

Where this leaves it. The structural case got stronger, not weaker: silicon cannot emit light, every optical link needs a III-V source, and the constraint on those sources is now being described by the biggest player in the field as worse than the memory shortage. Sivers has funded itself for roughly eleven quarters, cleared its debt, and secured partner capacity it does not pay to hold. What it has not shown is that recognised revenue becomes collected cash, and it spent a 52 minute call on everything except that line.

What would change my mind: the first quarter where contract assets fall while product revenue grows. Management will not need to say anything. The line will say it.
